Computer Tips & Tricks

Are you new to computers and want to learn the basics? This step-by-step guide explains how to set up a computer, connect to Wi-Fi, check your computer specifications, install apps, and perform other essential tasks.

Whether you are using a desktop computer or a laptop, these beginner-friendly instructions will help you get started and use your computer more confidently.

1. Computer Setup

Setting up a computer correctly is the first step for every beginner. Before using it, connect the necessary devices and check that everything is ready.

What You Need Before Starting

  • A desktop computer or laptop.
  • A monitor if you are using a desktop.
  • A keyboard and mouse for a desktop computer.
  • A suitable power cable or charger.
  • A safe power outlet or power strip.

How to Connect a Desktop Computer

  1. Place the computer and monitor on a stable desk with enough space for ventilation.
  2. Connect the monitor to the computer using a compatible HDMI, DisplayPort, or other video cable.
  3. Connect the keyboard and mouse to available USB ports.
  4. Connect the computer and monitor to suitable power outlets.
  5. Check that the cables are firmly connected and are not damaged.

If your monitor uses a separate power cable, make sure that it is connected too. Connecting the monitor's power cable alone will not connect it to the computer for displaying images.

What About a Laptop?

A laptop already has a built-in screen, keyboard, and touchpad. Connect its charger if needed, place it on a hard, flat surface, and make sure its ventilation openings are not blocked.

Check Everything Before Turning It On

Make sure the power connections are secure, the monitor is connected correctly, and the keyboard and mouse are ready to use. You can then proceed to turn on your computer.

2. Why Is a UPS Important for Your Computer?

A UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) provides temporary battery power when the main electricity supply fails. It can help you save your work and shut down your computer safely during a power interruption.

Why Should Beginners Consider Using a UPS?

  • It provides backup power for a limited time during a power cut.
  • It gives you time to save open documents and files.
  • It helps prevent unexpected shutdowns while you are working.
  • Some UPS models also provide protection against certain power disturbances.

How to Use a UPS

  1. Choose a UPS with a suitable power capacity for your computer and other essential devices.
  2. Read the manufacturer's instructions before connecting the equipment.
  3. Connect the computer and, if supported by the UPS capacity, the monitor to its battery-backed outlets.
  4. Charge and maintain the UPS according to the manufacturer's instructions.
  5. When electricity fails, save your work and shut down the computer before the backup battery runs out.

Can You Keep Working Until the Battery Is Empty?

No. Backup time depends on the UPS battery, its capacity, and the connected equipment. It is safer to save your work and shut down promptly when the power goes out.

Remember that a UPS does not provide unlimited backup power. It also does not replace regular backups of important files.

3. How to Connect Your Computer to Wi-Fi

A Wi-Fi connection lets your computer access the internet without a direct network cable. You usually need a working wireless network and its password.

Step 1: Open Settings

Click the Start button on your Windows computer and select Settings. You can also press Windows + I on your keyboard.

Step 2: Open Network & Internet

In the Settings window, select Network & Internet. On Windows 11, this option appears in the Settings navigation menu.

Step 3: Select Wi-Fi

Open the Wi-Fi section and make sure Wi-Fi is turned on. Select the available networks to see the Wi-Fi names around you.

Step 4: Choose Your Network and Enter the Password

  1. Select your home or office Wi-Fi network.
  2. Click Connect.
  3. Enter the correct Wi-Fi password.
  4. Click Next or continue with the on-screen instructions.

When the connection succeeds, Windows will show that you are connected to the network.

What to Do If Wi-Fi Is Not Connecting

  • Check that Wi-Fi is turned on.
  • Make sure you entered the correct password.
  • Move closer to the router if the signal is weak.
  • Restart the router if you are authorised to do so.
  • Disconnect and reconnect to the network.
  • If the Wi-Fi option is missing, check whether your computer has a wireless adapter and whether its driver is working.

How to Reduce Data Usage on a Mobile Hotspot

If you connect your computer through your phone's hotspot, Windows may use more mobile data than expected. You can reduce this by setting the network as metered.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Network & Internet, then Wi-Fi.
  3. Open your connected network's properties.
  4. Turn on Metered connection if the option is available.

A metered connection can help limit some automatic downloads, but it does not block all data usage. Windows menus may differ slightly by version.

4. How to Check Your Computer Specifications

Computer specifications tell you important details about your device, including its processor, RAM, Windows version, and system type. These details help you understand what your computer can do and whether it meets an app's requirements.

How to Check Specifications in Windows

  1. Click the Start button.
  2. Open Settings.
  3. Select System.
  4. Scroll down and choose About.
  5. Look under Device specifications and Windows specifications.

Important Details to Look For

  • Processor: The CPU that performs the computer's main processing tasks.
  • Installed RAM: Memory used by running programs and tasks.
  • System type: Shows whether Windows is 64-bit or 32-bit, among other system details.
  • Windows edition and version: Identifies the installed Windows release.

How to Check Storage Space

Open Settings, select System, and choose Storage. There you can review how much storage is used and how much space remains available.

You can also open File Explorer, select This PC, and view the available space on your drives.

Why Should You Know These Details?

Before installing software, compare your computer's specifications with the software's system requirements. This helps you avoid downloading programs that your device cannot run properly.

5. How to Download and Install Apps

Applications allow you to perform tasks such as browsing the internet, editing documents, attending online classes, and playing media files.

Method 1: Install an App from Microsoft Store

  1. Open the Start menu.
  2. Search for Microsoft Store and open it.
  3. Search for the app you need.
  4. Check the app name and publisher carefully.
  5. Select Get, Install, or the available installation option.
  6. Wait for the installation to finish, then open the app.

Method 2: Download from the Official Website

  1. Open a web browser such as Microsoft Edge.
  2. Visit the software developer's official website.
  3. Find the download page for the correct application.
  4. Check that the version supports your computer.
  5. Download the installer and wait for it to finish.
  6. Open the downloaded installer and follow the on-screen instructions.

Important Safety Tips

  • Download programs only from trusted sources.
  • Avoid suspicious advertisements and fake download buttons.
  • Read installation screens before clicking Next.
  • Avoid optional software that you do not need.
  • Keep Windows Security and your software updated.

What If an App Does Not Install?

Check your internet connection, available storage, system requirements, and any error message shown by the installer. Make sure you have permission to install the software on that computer.

6. How to Turn a Computer On and Off

Learning the correct way to start and shut down a computer is an essential skill for beginners. Proper shutdown helps prevent the loss of unsaved work.

How to Turn On a Desktop Computer

  1. Check that the power cables are connected properly.
  2. Turn on the monitor if it has a separate power button.
  3. Press the computer's power button once.
  4. Wait for Windows to load.
  5. Sign in if a password or PIN is required.

How to Turn On a Laptop

Open the laptop and press its power button. If the battery is empty, connect the charger and allow it to charge before trying again.

How to Shut Down Windows Properly

  1. Save your open documents and files.
  2. Close programs when you have finished using them.
  3. Click the Start button.
  4. Select the Power option.
  5. Choose Shut down.
  6. Wait until the computer has completely shut down.

Why Should You Avoid Force Shutdown?

Holding the power button to force a shutdown may cause unsaved work to be lost. Use this method only when the computer is unresponsive and normal shutdown is not possible.

Shut Down or Sleep: Which Should You Choose?

Sleep mode is useful when you plan to return to your work soon. Shut down the computer when you have finished using it for a longer period or when troubleshooting requires a restart or shutdown.

7. How to Use a Computer Mouse

A mouse helps you move the pointer, select items, open programs, and interact with Windows. Learning basic mouse operations makes computer use easier.

Basic Parts of a Mouse

  • Left button: Selects items and performs most clicking actions.
  • Right button: Opens a context menu with additional options.
  • Scroll wheel: Helps you move up and down a page.

Essential Mouse Tips for Beginners

  • Hold the mouse gently and keep your hand relaxed.
  • Place it on a flat surface or a suitable mouse pad.
  • Move the mouse smoothly to control the pointer.
  • Adjust pointer speed in Windows settings if needed.
  • Keep your wrist in a comfortable, neutral position.

Common Mouse Actions

  • Single-click: Usually selects an item.
  • Double-click: Usually opens a file or folder.
  • Right-click: Opens a menu of additional options.
  • Click and drag: Moves an item or selects text, depending on where you use it.
  • Scroll: Moves through a webpage or document.

How to Fix Common Mouse Problems

If the mouse does not respond, check its USB connection or wireless battery. Try another USB port if available, and ensure that the mouse is switched on if it is wireless.

If the pointer moves irregularly, clean the sensor gently and try using the mouse on a suitable surface. You can also check the pointer settings in Windows.
